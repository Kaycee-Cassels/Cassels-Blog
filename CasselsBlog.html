From Authentic Learning to Artificial Overreliance: How Generative Artificial Intelligence Negatively Impacts Cognitive & Creativity Skills in Students


With the rise of Artificial Intelligence (A.I.) in the early 2020's, its usage and reliance has integrated itself into most people's daily lives. Specifically, Generative A.I. has increasingly been evolving from just a mere language model to a necessary tool due to popular applications like OpenAI, Google Gemini, Claude, and many more.


So what exactly is Generative A.I.? This is a specific type of A.I. where the user inputs a prompt or command to the A.I., and it will generate a text, image, and/or video as the output. However, Gen A.I. doesn't actually "create" a brand new text, image, or video. Rather, it searches for existing ones on the web and combines any related images or videos that match the user's instructions.


Since Gen A.I. can instantly search for any specific piece of text, image, or video, it greatly reduces the search-time and work for the user. For students, this saves time on assignments, projects, and research since the Gen A.I. is mostly doing the work for them. While it is a very efficient tool, it also comes at a cost: their cognitivity and creativity.


When students go to A.I. agents like ChatGPT or Grok, they don't just ask for answers; they ask for the machines to think for them. From generating e-mails to drafting research outlines, the youth can ask the A.I. agent to do almost anything. And most of the time, the students will accept the A.I.'s output without checking for any errors or inaccuracies.


For example, Grassini, S. (2023) cautioned the propensity of Generative AI tools to fabricate so-called facts and generate false information convincingly. This may lead users to place undue trust in these technologies, escalating the risk of dependency. Such over-reliance could impede the cultivation of essential skills in students, including critical thinking, problem-solving, and effective communication.



Generative A.I. has also been integrated in education, further affecting student’s' reliance on A.I. since it is now also being encouraged in schools. Habitual overuse of AI for lesson planning, content generation, or assessment can erode critical thinking, reduce professional autonomy, and diminish intrinsic motivation. Such cognitive outsourcing may compromise professional judgment and decision-making, ultimately affecting teaching quality and learners’ experiences.


Koos and Wachsmann (2023) discussed the detrimental effects of over-reliance on the AI dialogue system, noting it may compromise the development of students’ critical thinking and problem-solving skills. A related study by Zhai et al (2024) argued that if students lean too heavily on AI for content generation, they risk not developing the ability to analyze information, construct logical arguments, or integrate knowledge from diverse sources for academic and professional success.


The overreliance on Generative A.I. has been shown to slowly decrease cognitive and creative skills in students since they outsource their knowledge to machines as means for efficiency. While Gen A.I. can help answer complex questions and draft assignments, it shouldn't be overused to the point where students aren't motivated to learn. Curiosity and creativity are a defining human trait which enriches our knowledge of the arts, literature, science, mathematics, and the world around us. By removing the motivation to learn, research, and create, and delegating the task towards A.I., it makes the youth more dependent on it. With all that’s been said, remember: Artificial Intelligence is only the pen, but not the writer.
